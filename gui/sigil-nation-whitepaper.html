One sentence

Every attested citizen of the SIGIL chain can claim a stipend at a fixed cadence, financed by a 2% carve of every block's mining reward, paid out in sUSD — SIGIL's collateral-backed stablecoin — so the safety net is denominated in certainty, not volatility.

No committee disburses it. No database can be edited to deny it. The stipend is a consensus rule: the same machinery that stops a double-spend is the machinery that guarantees the payment.

At activation the gross dev fee rises from 500 to 750 basis points. The welfare treasury takes 200 bps of the block reward, the master wallet nets 550, and the validator absorbs the 250 bps raise — deliberately: the safety net is paid for by the network's operators, not by squeezing the existing master split below its genesis rate. Every block below the activation height splits at the old genesis 500 — history replays byte-identically.

Why sUSD, not SIGIL

A stipend exists to be planned around. A citizen who receives 1 SIGIL receives a lottery ticket: worth one amount at claim time and a different amount at the grocery store. Paying in sUSD (operator ruling, 2026-08-31) makes the stipend a promise about purchasing power.

sUSD is not an IOU. It is minted only against native SIGIL locked in the collateral vault at the oracle price, with a 105% collateral cushion (MINT_BUFFER_BPS = 10_500). When a citizen claims, the treasury's SIGIL is locked as collateral and freshly minted sUSD lands in the citizen's wallet. If the treasury cannot cover the collateral, or the price oracle is unfed, the claim refuses — the system fails closed rather than paying with something it does not have.

Rollout honesty: the sUSD payout is implemented and committed (consensus commit 708fded3: WelfareClaim mints USDS; new OraclePush tx, tag 22, feeds the price oracle — which, until this work, had never had a production caller). It reaches the chain when the updated node binary is deployed and takes real effect once the authority pushes the first oracle price; until that price exists every claim refuses, by design. The live-status bar above tells you what the chain is doing right now.

Citizenship & privacy

Claims are open to attested citizens only. Attestation (CitizenAttest, tx tag 20) is signed by the nation's authority wallet — the master wallet, whose key actually exists and is genesis-pinned. What goes on chain is not your civil ID: the wallet hashes it in your browser (SHA-256("sigil-cpr|" + id)) and only that hash enters the borger registry. The chain can prove "this wallet is a citizen" without ever learning who the citizen is.

A claim (WelfareClaim, tx tag 21) needs no starting balance — the transaction fee comes out of the stipend itself, so a citizen with zero SIGIL can still claim. That detail is the whole point: a welfare system you need money to access is not a welfare system.

What this is, structurally

Most chains ask "how do we stop bad transactions?" This module asks the inverse: can a chain make a payment obligation as tamper-proof as a balance? The stipend is not a treasury someone administers — it is a standing consequence of block production. As long as blocks are mined, the treasury fills; as long as the treasury covers the collateral, any citizen's claim is valid by rule. The state cannot quietly skip a payment for the same reason it cannot quietly mint a coin.
