\section{Introduction: why reinvent money, and why a chain needs an action}

Money is the technology we are least allowed to get wrong and the one we most take on
faith. Its supply is set by discretion, its settlement runs through intermediaries we are
required to trust, and the record of who owns what is a private ledger we are shown, not
one we can check. Bitcoin's founding insight was that the last property is not a law of
nature: a network can agree on a shared ledger with no trusted keeper, and a supply no one
can quietly inflate. That insight is now more than fifteen years proven — and more than
fifteen years old, and the chains built on it each stopped short of the whole problem.

Bitcoin fixed the money and left the expressiveness: a capped, honest supply and an
unforgeable ledger, but deliberately no contracts, no native exchange, no programmable
economy — and, like every widely deployed chain, elliptic-curve signatures that a
large-scale quantum computer would break. Ethereum added general-purpose
programmable state and \emph{already} commits state, transaction, and receipt roots in
every execution header. What it does not provide is a node that must \emph{halt} on a
root mismatch rather than continue, a fixed 21M-style monetary policy, a post-quantum
floor under its signatures, or protocol-level provenance for its released client
binaries. So SIGIL's distinction is not ``a chain with a state root'' — it is the
\emph{combination} neither puts in the protocol: domain-separated roots with fail-closed
local enforcement, a post-quantum signature floor, build provenance bound to every
released binary, and time-normalized capped emission.
SIGIL — the experimental sister chain to Quillon Graph — takes the obvious brief: keep
what they got right (capped honest money, programmability), add what post-quantum
cryptography and a self-signing compiler now make possible, and lose what they got wrong.

Concretely, SIGIL is a chain where:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2.5pt,label=\textcolor{sigilcyan}{$\diamond$}]
\item \textbf{Post-quantum runs from the floor up.} Every release binary carries a
\emph{require-both} SQIsign Level-5 \emph{and} Ed25519 provenance proof (\textasciitilde356\,B,
smaller than one Dilithium-5 signature); the whole chain folds to one constant-size
Ajtai/SIS proof with no trusted setup; browser key material is ML-KEM-1024 / ML-DSA-87.
\item \textbf{Agreement is structural.} Every header commits four state roots — wallet,
DEX, event log, contract storage — and a node that drifts on any of them halts itself
(exit 78) before it serves a byte. Divergence cannot hide.
\item \textbf{The economy is programmable.} A contract VM, a constant-product AMM DEX
($x\cdot y = k$), on-chain credit, and a Bitcoin bridge — each committing to its own state
root, so the whole programmable economy is inside the structural guarantee, not bolted
beside it.
\item \textbf{It verifies on a potato.} A 2{,}568-byte whole-chain proof and a
3\,$\mu$s browser tip-check; the light client downloads zero blocks to hold the tip to
account.
\item \textbf{The money stays honest.} Dual-lane mining (BLAKE-family PoW $\times$
Wesolowski VDF), a 21M cap, time-based emission invariant under block cadence, and a
fail-loud enforcer that halts the node at $\pm$2\% supply drift.
\item \textbf{One static binary} is node, miner, light client, and wallet.
\end{itemize}

None of that is new as a \emph{list} — and a feature list is exactly what SIGIL is trying
\emph{not} to be. A blockchain is usually presented as a pile of mechanisms: a hash here,
a signature there, a consensus rule, an emission curve, each justified on its own and the
whole trusted because the parts individually check out. SIGIL is built on the opposite
conviction: that a chain should follow from a single organizing object, the way a physical
theory follows from an \emph{action}, and that agreement about state should be
\emph{structural} — written into the object every node checks, not reassembled from
conventions each node is merely trusted to honor. That is why \emph{what the network agrees
the state is} became a first-class, committed quantity (the four roots above): agreement
stops being faith in a linear ledger and becomes a checkable property of the header itself.

The v0 whitepaper (2026-05-29) stated these as engineering claims. What v0 lacked — and
what Quillon's own quantum-physics whitepaper demonstrated the value of — is a
\emph{unifying theoretical object} from which the design follows, rather than a list of
mechanisms. That organizing object has now been proposed: a conjectural effective action
$\delta\SSIGIL=0$ in which every protocol component — settlement conservation, emission
control, witness attestation, fork topology, the post-quantum floor — corresponds to one
field. It was developed in the SIGIL master-equation programme (2026-05-30/31) using the
\texttt{flux-science} modules already in the workspace, and this edition finally puts it
where it was always intended:\ first (full companion note published standalone; provenance
of every measured claim: Appendix~\ref{app:meq}).

\subsection{Correspondences: one field per protocol component}

Each variation of $\SSIGIL$ with respect to one field \emph{names} one subsystem of the
running chain. The paper's central organizational claim is deliberately modest:
\emph{SIGIL is designed as if it were one action varied six ways}, and that discipline —
every new mechanism must say which term it belongs to — is what the equation buys
today. What it does not yet buy is derivation: the component Lagrangians
($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{settle}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{witness}}$,
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{topo}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{crypto}}$) are unspecified beyond
their field content, and no discretization of $\mathcal{G}$ into a lattice field theory
has been constructed, so the ``field equations'' below are \emph{correspondences}
between standard equations and shipped mechanisms, not consequences extracted from the
action. Making even one of them a genuine derivation is the physics programme's headline
open problem (\S\ref{sec:honesty}).

\layman{sigilviolet}{``correspondence, not derivation''}{We are saying the protocol
\emph{rhymes} with famous physics — not that we've done the mathematics forcing it to.
Think of a city map drawn in pencil: genuinely useful for navigating, honest about
being redrawable. Each pencil line gets inked only when a measurement makes it earn
the ink. One line already has (\S\ref{sec:delivery}); the rest are queued on the
scoreboard (\S\ref{sec:predictions}).}

\nabla_\mu J^\mu = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\partial_t \rho_{\mathrm{value}} + \nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{settle}}
= S_{\mathrm{emission}}(\varphi)
\end{equation}
Value is neither created nor destroyed except by the emission source term. In code this
is the \path{commit_state_transition} chokepoint (\S\ref{sec:arch}): the \emph{only}
function permitted to mutate chain state, with conservation checked per typed delta.
The 21M supply cap is the integral form; the live laboratory network reads 21{,}000{,}000
of 21{,}000{,}000 SIGIL minted under its accelerated test economics.

\paragraph{$\delta\SSIGIL/\delta\varphi = 0$ — Klein–Gordon emission (monetary policy).}
\begin{equation}
\ddot{\varphi} + 3H\dot{\varphi} + V'(\varphi) = 0,\qquad
V(\varphi) = K_p\,e^2 + K_i\,e\!\!\int\! e\,dt + K_d\,(\dot e)^2
\end{equation}
The PID emission controller is \emph{cast as} a scalar field rolling in a potential;
the Hubble friction term $3H\dot\varphi$ is network expansion (new blocks arriving).
Inflationary cosmology as a mnemonic for economic policy. The displayed $V$ is
\emph{schematic}: $e$, $\int e\,dt$, and $\dot e$ are controller states, not functions
of $\varphi$ alone, so $V$ is not an ordinary local potential — a faithful treatment
introduces auxiliary fields (or defines $e(\varphi)$ explicitly). The mapping is exact
only for quadratic potentials. Stability prediction, from the linearized characteristic
equation $K_d s^2 + K_p s + K_i = 0$: the controller is non-oscillatory iff
$K_p^2 \ge 4K_dK_i$ (\S\ref{sec:predictions}; this corrects the discriminant stated in
the v0.1 master-equation note, which had the roles of $K_p$ and $K_d$ interchanged — a
fresh derivation against the implemented controller is part of the test).

\paragraph{$\delta\SSIGIL/\delta K = 0$ — topological invariance (fork detection).}
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dt}\,V_K(q)\Big|_{\mathrm{consensus}} = 0
\end{equation}
where $K$ is the link obtained by closing the DAG's braid over a bounded window. Valid
consensus moves are Reidemeister moves on the braid; invariants are conserved. A fork
\emph{may} change the bounded-window invariant — when it does, divergence is proven;
equality of invariants never proves equality of histories. \textbf{Two honesty notes.}
\emph{Computational:} the Jones polynomial is \#P-hard in general — nobody runs ``Jones
consensus at 10k blocks/s.'' SIGIL's tractable programme (QTFT lanes) commits a
\emph{cheap} invariant per block over a bounded window — linking number
($O(\mathrm{crossings})$) or the Alexander polynomial (polynomial-time via the Burau
determinant) — reserving Jones/Khovanov machinery for rare deep-fork adjudication off
the hot path. \emph{Logical:} cheap invariants fingerprint, they do not classify; the
topology check is a one-way tripwire complementing (never replacing) the state-root
comparison. The braid ordering layer this attaches to is live: the DagKnight braid
shipped to the live experimental network's producer 2026-07-02 (\S\ref{sec:dagknight}).

\paragraph{$\delta\SSIGIL/\delta A^\mu = 0$ — Yang–Mills witnesses (divergence
detection).}
\begin{equation}
\nabla_\nu F^{\mu\nu} = J^\mu_{\mathrm{witness}},\qquad F = dA + A\wedge A
\end{equation}
When two state-root tracks diverge, the curvature $F$ becomes locally non-zero;
divergence detection is the measurement of $|F|$ above threshold. (Healing is
\emph{re-synchronization dynamics} that drives $F$ back toward zero — not a gauge
transformation, which merely relabels the connection and cannot repair genuine
disagreement; curvature is gauge-covariant.) In code: four state roots per header,
computed independently by every node, with mismatch $\Rightarrow$ self-halt (exit code
78, no auto-restart). The adversarial simulation gate measured divergence $=0$ across
all six scenarios (\S\ref{sec:dagknight}) — the flat-connection condition holds on the
paths tested.

per-signature verification but a change of $\Sigma_{\mathrm{hash}}$ collapsing
whole-chain verification to $O(1)$ (\S\ref{sec:fold}). \emph{The wall was a
cryptographic choice, not a hardware limit} — and it moved.

\subsection{The method: Variational Flow \hfill \stL}\label{sec:varflow}

Every law below was produced under one discipline. \textbf{VarFlow --- Variational Flow ---
is SIGIL's change discipline.} A proposal names the single action term it varies,
preregisters a numerical prediction and failure condition, measures the live path, grades
evidence and deployment separately, and is then shipped, revised or rejected. Every accepted
result must produce both an operational rule and the next experiment that could falsify it.
VarFlow applies the same rule to itself: if its preregistered usefulness criteria fail, the
methodology must evolve or retire.

\layman{sigilcyan}{Why the results are graded, not just reported}{Anyone can post a
benchmark. VarFlow forces the number to be \emph{predicted before it is measured}, with a
line drawn in advance for what would count as failure --- so a result cannot be quietly
reframed after the fact. The two-axis grade (how strong the evidence is, and whether it is
actually deployed) is part of the method, not an afterthought; it is why nothing in this
section can be read as more settled than it is.}

\subsection{The gossip delivery law \hfill \stM}\label{sec:delivery}

For a block gossiped with redundancy $r$ under independent per-transmission loss
probability $p$, the probability a node receives it at least once is

\begin{equation}
\boxed{\,D(p,r) = 1 - p^{\,r}\,}
\qquad\text{inverted to the provisioning rule}\qquad
r^{*} = \left\lceil \frac{\ln(1-\mathrm{SLA})}{\ln p} \right\rceil
\end{equation}

Derived under an independent-loss assumption and validated against 22 seeded
flux-chronos star-flood simulations (RMS residual 0.4\,pp) — and, as of v1.3,
\textbf{measured on a real lossy network}. The v1.2 regrade demanded an
independently controlled experiment; it was run 2026-07-22: the shipped
request/response primitive over real TCP against 8 real peers, kernel netem loss in
both directions, 9{,}000 trials / 18{,}000 attempts, with $\hat p$ \emph{measured}
per run (never assumed) and the composition tested at $r{=}2,3$. Within-run RMS
residual \measured{1.50 percentage points} over the six informative cells. Two
findings sharpen the law's meaning: (i) the law's $p$ lives at the \emph{request}
level, not the packet level — TCP absorbs $\le$10\% packet loss entirely
($\hat p = 0$ over 3{,}600 attempts), so operators must provision $r^{*}$ from
measured request-failure rates; (ii) per-link Gilbert--Elliott burst loss does
\emph{not} break cross-peer composition ($+1.3/{+}1.5$\,pp), while transport
collapse ($\hat p\approx0.94$) pushes delivery below independence ($-3$\,pp) —
both in the directions the limitations section anticipated. Evidence
\textsc{measured} (controlled real-network experiment; WAN live-mesh extension
open), deployment \emph{live} ($r=3$ default). Record:
\texttt{docs/research/delivery-law-live/}. The arc now demonstrates the full
discipline: model, simulation campaign, closed form, residual, provisioning rule,
regrade under review, and the promotion experiment the regrade demanded. (Standalone paper published at
\url{https://quillon.xyz/downloads/sigil-top-delivery-law.pdf}.)

\layman{sigilcyan}{the delivery law}{If a letter gets lost one time in ten, send three
copies — the chance all three vanish is one in a thousand. That's the entire law:
$1-p^r$. We didn't guess it: 22 simulated storms matched it to half a percentage
point, and then — because the simulation shared the formula's own assumption — we
rebuilt the storm for real: real network stack, the kernel itself dropping packets,
nine thousand deliveries counted. The formula held to within 1.5 percentage points
everywhere the network still functioned, and we learned two honest things the
simulation couldn't teach: losses that matter are lost \emph{requests}, not lost
packets, and when the network truly collapses, three copies stop saving you. The
network \emph{calculates} how many copies to send — from measured numbers.}

on our benchmark); after that, each new page costs three \emph{millionths} of a second.
Your phone — honestly, a potato — can hold the chain to account.}

\subsection{The sync ladder and the honest bottleneck}\label{sec:sync}

The throughput programme is a case study in the ``measure the live path first'' rule.
Local pipeline stages benchmark at 251k--2.5M blocks/s and a flat append-only skeleton
store (72\,B/record, two-phase durable, torn-tail safe) sustains $\sim$10M records/s
durable — $2600\times$ the general-purpose LSM write path ($\sim$130\,$\mu$s/KV
$\approx$ 3.9k blocks/s). An in-process end-to-end harness (pull $\to$ stream-verify
$\to$ commit at 2M records, roots matching) reaches 39k blocks/s. And yet the live sync
number sat at $\sim$800 blocks/s for five days — because the actual caps were a
producer-side full-block serve throttle ($\le$1 per 120\,ms) and a serial
request$\to$await$\to$apply fetch loop, \emph{not} the local pipeline. Once measured
directly, both were removed: the serve throttle is gone (finalized ranges are
cache-served by \texttt{memcpy}) and a request-ahead window (up to $\sim$16 ranges in
flight, apply-backpressured) replaced the serial loop — \measured{live 2{,}322 $\to$
10{,}853 blocks/s} on the running node. The lesson is now Rule 0 of the SIGIL skill and a
methodological claim of this paper: \textbf{a fast microbenchmark next to a slow live
number is a bug report about your understanding, not a victory} — and the fix is the one
that moves the \emph{live} number, which this one did. (Snapshot-pull, a further
$100\times$ path, is coded and byte-symmetric but stays gated off pending a published
signed anchor; \S\ref{sec:honesty}.) Wire compression is measured at 14.0$\times$ (zstd
backfill codec, 70.7\,B/header live) and 26.3$\times$ (gossip frames).

\subsection{Holding the project to account: commitment provenance \hfill \stG}

The same discipline turns on the project itself. SIGIL signs its \emph{binaries} but,
until recently, its \emph{promises} --- roadmap commitments, security disclosures, the
``single-producer today'' admission, the reset-before-mainnet pledge --- carried no
provenance at all. A community reader made the point sharply: a chain built to hold its
ledger to account should be able to hold its own word to account, by the same means. It
can, and it needs no new cryptographic primitive --- only careful composition, data
availability, and an \emph{independent} witness.

A \emph{Commitment Record} is a canonical, content-addressed statement --- atomic (one
stable id per promise: \texttt{mainnet.reset-before-launch}, \texttt{allocation.none},

so no past record can be silently inserted, reordered, or deleted (a change of direction
\emph{supersedes}, never erases). Its digest is anchored in the block's event-log root.
But a single-producer, resettable chain cannot independently witness its own \emph{time}
--- the same hand holds the promise key, the record chain, and the anchoring chain. The
break in that circle is an external witness: each record head is Bitcoin-timestamped via
OpenTimestamps, so the date is attested by an authority we do not control.

\subsection{Dual-lane mining: $\Phi$ POWER $\times$ $\Omega$ TIME \hfill \stL}
\label{sec:mining}

A valid share carries \emph{both} lanes: $\Phi$ — BLAKE4 proof-of-work, and $\Omega$ —
a Wesolowski VDF proof of elapsed sequential time. \emph{BLAKE4} is SIGIL's name for
its parameterized proof-of-work construction built from the BLAKE3 compression function
— not a newly established cryptographic hash. The round count is the parameter: at
$R=7$ it is KAT-verified byte-identical to BLAKE3 (inheriting BLAKE3's security
analysis wholesale); reduced-round variants are an experimental, measured speed lever
($2.9\times$ at $R=1$, scalar) with no security claim attached. Consensus stays at
$R=7$ unless a reduced round count is ever cryptanalytically validated. The TUI miner
and the node share one verification function and one cap-enforced crediting chokepoint;
the loop is proven live (first credited shares 0\,$\to$\,1250 on \texttt{sigil-g0}).

\layman{sigilcyan}{proof of work $\times$ proof of time}{A valid share must show two
different things: that you \emph{burned effort} (a hash puzzle) and that you
\emph{genuinely waited} (a sequential timer that cannot be fast-forwarded — not even
with a warehouse of GPUs). Effort can be bought in bulk; honest elapsed time cannot.
A block needs both, which is why the two lanes are multiplied, not added.}

\subsection{What's real (with evidence)}

\subsection{What's still pretend (physics)}

The headline gap first: $\SSIGIL$ is a \emph{conjectural effective action}. Its
component Lagrangians are unspecified beyond field content, no discretization of the
BlockDAG into a lattice field theory has been constructed, and consequently none of the
\S\ref{sec:action} correspondences is currently a derivation. The reputation-induced
metric $g_{\mu\nu}$ is a heuristic — the functional form awaits fitting to measured
consensus drift. Jones-polynomial machinery is \#P-hard; only the bounded-window
linking-number/Alexander programme is claimed tractable, cheap invariants are one-way
tripwires (they fingerprint, not classify), and the header topology commitment is
staged, not shipped. AdS/CFT on a discrete graph is an analogy pending rigorous
discretization. The PID/Klein–Gordon mapping is schematic (controller states are not
local in $\varphi$) and exact only for quadratic potentials. The delivery law — the
programme's best result — is now measured on a real lossy network (v1.3; netem
experiment, within-run RMS 1.50\,pp), with the honest scope attached: a controlled
single-host namespace lab with minimal real-protocol responders, not the WAN-scale
live mesh, and the law is known to under-predict failure clustering at transport
collapse ($-3$\,pp at $\hat p\approx0.94$). The layered-hardness
security design states intent per property; a joint security model with a formal
composition bound is open, as is the fold's concrete soundness reduction.

\subsection{What's still pretend (engineering)}

The RPC edge is authenticated (per-request wallet signature + nonce; the audit's theft
findings are fixed and enforcing), so the residual money risk is deeper. The
highest-severity open item is \textbf{H1}: block-apply does not yet enforce the producer
signature — a height-gated verifier is implemented and unit-tested on a branch but dormant,
not deployed. Two more sit at the consensus-crypto layer with their fix half-built: the VDF
still runs over a forgeable fixed modulus though a trustless replacement exists unwired
(\textbf{C10}), and the state-layer replay-nonce primitive is committed + signed but not yet
called on the live apply path (\textbf{H5}). Peer-sync ingress is under-authenticated: a
length-only handshake stub (\textbf{H2}), a follower that trusts peer-declared difficulty
(\textbf{H3}), and keyless wire tip-proofs (\textbf{H4}). Each carries a verdict
(fixed / partial / open) with file:line in the hardening backlog
(\texttt{docs/SIGIL\_HARDENING\_BACKLOG.md}) — that document, ground-truthed against the
code rather than the stale audit, is what the ``open'' rung of Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}
points at. (The audit's C9 bridge-mint and H6 event-root findings were re-verified as
already fixed.) Snapshot-pull live activation awaits a
pinned signed anchor on the fleet default plus its M2 crypto (SQIsign + fold in the
trailer); until then nodes fall through to the compatible crawl path. The serve-throttle
and serial-fetch caps of \S\ref{sec:sync} were fixed (throttle removed, request-ahead
window live); the remaining \texttt{sigil-top} adaptive multi-substream scheduler is coded
but dark.
