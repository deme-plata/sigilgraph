[b][size=13pt]Why SIGIL[/size][/b]

Money is the one thing we're least allowed to get wrong and the one we most take on faith — discretionary supply, settlement through intermediaries, a ledger we're shown instead of one we can check. Bitcoin proved the last part isn't a law of nature. But the chains built on that insight each stopped short:

[list]
[*][b]Bitcoin[/b] fixed the money and left the expressiveness — capped honest supply, unforgeable ledger, but no contracts, no native exchange, and elliptic-curve signatures a quantum computer would break.
[*][b]Ethereum[/b] fixed the expressiveness and loosened the guarantees — contracts and on-chain markets, but a header that makes no structural statement of what the world-state IS, supply and provenance enforced by process not primitive, and the same pre-quantum signatures underneath.
[/list]

Both share three gaps SIGIL exists to close: nothing makes state [b]divergence[/b] self-evident, nothing puts a [b]post-quantum[/b] floor under the whole stack, and nothing binds the [b]running binary[/b] to the source it was built from. The brief: keep what they got right (capped honest money, programmability), add what post-quantum crypto and a self-signing compiler make possible, lose what they got wrong.

Here is what that buys you, and it is all live on the testnet today:

[list]
[*]🔐 [b]The compiler signs what it builds.[/b] Every release binary carries a post-quantum provenance proof emitted at build time — require-both [b]SQIsign Level 5 AND Ed25519[/b] over a canonical record binding the artifact hash, source hash, compiler version and builder identity. ~356 bytes total, smaller than a single Dilithium-5 signature (4,595 B). A stripped single-leg bundle is rejected, never downgraded. You verify the download yourself (walkthrough below) — that's the whole point.
[*]📦 [b]Four state roots in every header[/b] (wallet / dex / event-log / contract). A node that drifts on any root diverges on the header and [b]halts itself[/b] (exit 78) before it serves a single byte. State divergence has nowhere to hide.
[*]⚙️ [b]A programmable economy, inside the guarantee.[/b] A contract [b]VM[/b], a constant-product [b]AMM DEX[/b] (x·y=k), on-chain [b]credit[/b], and a [b]Bitcoin bridge[/b] — each committing to its own state root, so the whole economy lives under the structural guarantee instead of bolted beside it.
[*]🔎 [b]One constant-size proof for the whole chain.[/b] The fold (Ajtai/SIS lattice commitments, no trusted setup): [b]2,568 bytes regardless of chain length[/b] — full verification 280–342 ms at the 100k-block benchmark, and a [b]3-microsecond[/b] incremental tip check running live on the ~29.9M-block network. Honest nuance: constant-size proof, not constant-cost verification — the incremental lane is what makes the steady state cheap.
[*]💻 [b]One binary does everything, on a potato.[/b] sigil-top (Linux x86_64 + Windows x64) is a monitoring node, a light client, a dual-lane miner, and a wallet in a single static file. Default mode verifies the live tip fingerprint in ~3 µs with zero blocks downloaded. Opt-in full sync runs at a measured live 10,853 blocks/s.
[*]📡 [b]A measured gossip law, not a guess.[/b] Delivery probability D(p,r) = 1 − p^r, validated against 22 seeded simulations (RMS residual 0.4 pp). The shipped redundancy r=3 clears a 99.9% delivery SLA at up to 10% packet loss — the network calculates its provisioning instead of hoping.
[*]🕸️ [b]Adversarially gated ordering.[/b] The DagKnight braid passed a six-scenario gate before the live producer adopted it: divergence = 0 under 30% drop/reorder/equivocation, a withheld-block attacker, 16/16 ordering permutations agree, tamper suite 5/5 rejected. Simulation evidence, not a theorem — and we label which is which.
[*]₿ [b]Bitcoin-familiar economics.[/b] 21M cap, 4-year halvings — but time-based: block reward = the integral of the emission rate over the block interval, so the schedule is invariant under block cadence, a compile-time assert proves it sums exactly to the cap, and a fail-loud enforcer halts the node at ±2% supply drift.
[/list]

[b][size=13pt]The plan: testnet now, mainnet when it earns it[/size][/b]

We are on testnet [b]on purpose[/b]. The claims above — a post-quantum provenance chain, a constant-size whole-chain proof, structural agreement — are exactly the kind that should be attacked by strangers before anything of value depends on them. So the road to a production mainnet is public and gated on closing a short, named list of hardening items (block-apply producer-signature enforcement, the trustless VDF modulus, peer-sync ingress authentication), each tracked with file:line status in an open backlog. When they close and the chain has soaked, mainnet follows. Until then: break it, and tell us how.

[b][size=13pt]Mining[/size][/b]

Dual-lane: a hash-power lane (BLAKE3-family PoW) × a verifiable-delay lane (Wesolowski VDF) — a valid share carries both. CPU is fine; a GPU build is available for the hash lane. Verified wallets (sign a domain-separated challenge — the wallet IS the public key) earn to themselves; unverified mining routes to the treasury. 1.2% of every reward routes to a commons pool under an M-of-N quorum.

[b][size=13pt]What we are NOT claiming[/size][/b]

The honesty section is load-bearing — the whitepaper carries a boxed security disclosure and a public hardening backlog with file:line status. The short version:

[list]
[*]RPC money routes ARE authenticated (per-wallet ed25519 signature + nonce). The residual risk is deeper, and named: block-apply does not yet enforce the producer's signature (verifier written + height-gated, dormant); the live VDF modulus is not yet the trustless one (drop-in built, height-gated cutover pending); peer-sync ingress auth is mid-rollout (a real ed25519 verify-before-sync handshake with channel binding landed in log-only mode). [b]Do not route real value through SIGIL.[/b] It's a testnet; it will be reset.
[*]The multi-million-tx/s figures in our docs are state-transition wind-tunnel numbers, not network TPS, and are labeled as such everywhere.
[*]The fold construction is ours and unpublished; its soundness reduction (Ajtai/SIS) is a target, not a published proof. Cryptanalysis is explicitly invited — that's half the reason this post exists.
[/list]
